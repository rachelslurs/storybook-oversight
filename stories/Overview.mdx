## Why these are rules

`@storybook/addon-mcp` already lets you _view_ the raw manifest. Three checks in this demo need judgment that reading it can't give you:

- `extractor-drift` is a comparison. The manifest looks fine on its own; it's only wrong relative to the extractor you expected.
- `docs-link-dangling` takes every id in the manifest to know a `?path=` redirect points at nothing. One component's entry can't tell (the panel strikes the dead link through where it appears in the description).
- `required-prop-undocumented` vs `prop-descriptions-missing`: every blank prop reads the same in a raw view. Oversight calls the required one an `error`, because that's the gap an agent is most likely to guess through.

A fourth, `prop-shape-unrecognized`, needs a manifest whose prop payload has moved, which this demo can't stage. [Why these are lint rules](https://github.com/rachelslurs/storybook-oversight/blob/main/docs/why-lint-rules.md) makes the whole argument.

## The manifest-level rule

`extractor-drift` isn't tied to one component. It fires when the manifest was built with a different docgen extractor than the one you state as `expectedExtractor` (addon config, or `--expected-extractor` in the CLI); with no expectation stated, the rule does not run. The shallow `react-docgen` parser silently drops complex prop types, so descriptions come back empty across the board. Because it's a property of the whole manifest, the panel reports it separately:

## Not shown here

`docgen-missing` and `story-extraction-error` fire only on extraction failures, which can't be staged with well-formed components. They're covered by the addon's unit tests instead.
